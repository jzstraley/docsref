Cardiac computed tomography provides high-resolution anatomic assessment of the coronary arteries, cardiac structures, great vessels, and surrounding thorax. Its principal strengths are coronary artery evaluation, calcium quantification, and procedural planning.

## Core Strengths

Cardiac CT is particularly useful for evaluating:

- Coronary artery anatomy and stenosis
- Coronary artery calcium
- Atherosclerotic plaque
- Anomalous coronary arteries
- Coronary bypass grafts
- Selected coronary stents
- Left atrial appendage anatomy and thrombus
- Pulmonary vein anatomy
- Structural heart interventions
- Aortic disease
- Congenital heart disease
- Cardiac masses
- Pericardial anatomy

## How Cardiac CT Answers Clinical Questions

| Clinical question | Principal CT technique |
|---|---|
| Is coronary atherosclerosis present? | Coronary calcium scoring |
| Is obstructive CAD present? | Coronary CT angiography |
| Is an intermediate stenosis flow-limiting? | CT-derived fractional flow reserve |
| What are the plaque characteristics? | CCTA plaque assessment |
| Is a coronary anomaly present? | CCTA with 3D reconstruction |
| Is left atrial appendage thrombus present? | Early and delayed-phase CT |
| What is the pulmonary vein anatomy? | ECG-gated left atrial CT |
| Is the patient anatomically suitable for TAVR? | TAVR planning CT |
| What is the extent of aortic disease? | ECG-gated or nongated CT angiography |
| Is the pericardium thickened or calcified? | Cardiac or chest CT |

## Major Limitations

- Ionizing radiation
- Iodinated contrast exposure
- Image degradation from motion or arrhythmia
- Reduced coronary interpretability with extensive calcification
- Limited functional and tissue characterization compared with CMR
- Heart-rate control may be required
- Breath-holding is necessary for optimal image quality
- Obesity can reduce image quality and increase radiation exposure
- CT stenosis severity does not always establish physiologic significance
